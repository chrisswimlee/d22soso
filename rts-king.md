## The Dawn

Long before sold-out esports arenas filled stadiums in Seoul or multi-million dollar streaming contracts transformed digital competition into global entertainment, the internet gaming landscape was an uncharted frontier. In the late 1990s, three elements collided to spark a revolution: Kali (an early IPX network emulator enabling legacy multiplayer games to work over modern TCP/IP), competitive ranking structures like Case’s Ladder, and the real-time strategy (RTS) games themselves.

At the center of this transformation was Wayne Chiang, known online as SoSOWAC (and later D22-soso). Before he became the first-ever *StarCraft: Brood War* World Champion, Chiang laid the concrete foundation for competitive gaming across *Command & Conquer* and *Warcraft II*, while keeping a footprint on *Magic: The Gathering*—earning a reputation as a pioneer whose strategic mind defined an era and echoes to today. After being introduced to competitive Magic through legend Brian Weissman, Chiang found his true calling.

## 1996 — The Dawn of RTS: Command & Conquer

*Command & Conquer: Tiberian Dawn* (1995) forged the blueprint for modern digital strategy. Through Kali, Wayne “SoSOWAC” Chiang shot up the ranks on Case’s Ladder, mentored by Case’s Ladder co-owner Bill “Bill1” Macaitis. Chiang defined C&C gameplay and the entire metagame: his team Synergy held the #1 ranking on the Command & Conquer Case’s Team Ladder while they were active.

## 1997 — Escalation: Warcraft II

*Warcraft II: Tides of Darkness* (1995) elevated fantasy strategy with fog-of-war mechanics, naval combat, and macro management. Chiang transitioned to Kali’s competitive circuit. Joining Clan C4L, he achieved Top 3 rankings in 1v1 play and held the #1 team spot. He first met rivals and future teammates Jay “Gadianton” Severson (co-founder, Chess.com).

## 1998–2000 — StarCraft: Brood War First World Champion

On March 31, 1998, Blizzard Entertainment released *StarCraft*, permanently changing competitive gaming. Chiang immediately captured the #1 spot on Case’s Ladder in both 1v1 and team divisions, winning the inaugural local StarCraft tournament at Neutral Ground in Mountain View, California against his longtime rival Jay Severson.

Transitioning into community building, Chiang joined forces with esports figures Dennis “Thresh” Fong and Bob “Calbear” Colayco at Gamers.com running GX StarCraft. Adopting the tag D22-soso, Chiang served as StarCraft’s first tournament caster during PGL Season 3, covering the international tournaments, authored battle reports immediately after tournament and ladder matches analyzing top-tier ladder play, and pioneered community media concepts like *Pimpest Plays*—the structural blueprint for modern esports highlight reels.

## 2001–2002 — Magic: The Gathering — Chiang’s Esports Finale

Concurrently with real-time strategy dominance, Chiang engaged in high-level play in *Magic: The Gathering*. Early encounters with Pro Tour titan Brian Weissman at Palo Alto hotspot Round Table Pizza helped sharpen Chiang’s understanding of card advantage, resource efficiency, and tempo.

Although Chiang treated Magic primarily as a side game, he consistently made Top 8 appearances at PTQs and developed early custom formats like “The Cube,” which is now the modern Cube Draft format—dynamic, cost-effective draft environments.

After moving on from competitive real-time strategy, Wayne Chiang qualified for three Magic: The Gathering Pro Tours, attending Pro Tour New York 2001 and Pro Tour Boston 2002. The Cube would eventually become one of the most beloved *Magic: The Gathering* formats of all time and a staple on Magic Online.
